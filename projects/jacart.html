JACart Autonomous Vehicle / Multimodal Anomaly Detection

Integrating sensors, localization health, vehicle telemetry, and camera context into an operator-facing anomaly-detection workflow for JMU's autonomous research platform.

Problem

An autonomous platform needs context when something goes wrong.

JACart combines networked sensors, ROS 2 nodes, localization, autonomy logic, and operator workflows. A failure rarely belongs to one component: stale localization, missing point clouds, camera context, network behavior, or an unhealthy service can change what the vehicle is able to do.

The research problem was to improve visibility into abnormal operating conditions by combining vehicle telemetry and localization context with camera information, then using locally served multimodal models to support analysis without depending on a remote inference service.

Architecture

Build one traceable path from sensors to operator insight.

Velodyne LiDAR, ZED cameras, and GPS/IMU inputs enter the ROS 2 sensor pipeline. MOLA consumes relevant sensor data for localization, and localization-health signals join the wider vehicle state. The anomaly workflow combines that telemetry with event-relevant camera context, sends a bounded analysis request to a locally served LLM/VLM through Ollama, and produces artifacts that an operator can inspect.

What I personally owned

Hands-on integration plus planning across the research system.

  • Developed the multimodal anomaly-detection workflow combining telemetry, localization health, and camera context.
  • Integrated locally served multimodal-model analysis through Ollama.
  • Debugged ROS 2 sensor and service behavior across LiDAR, ZED cameras, localization, Dockerized services, and networked components.
  • Supported the migration from NDT localization to MOLA ICP and incorporated localization-health signals into the anomaly path.
  • Led technical planning, milestones, testing priorities, documentation, coordination, and onboarding across subteams.

Scope boundary

I integrated locally served LLM/VLM capabilities into the monitoring workflow. I do not claim to have trained or fine-tuned a JACart foundation model.

Implementation

Progression from subsystem work to whole-platform responsibility.

Studied and tested risk across networked vehicle components, building familiarity with the complete platform rather than one isolated node.

Vehicle development

Worked on ROS 2 migration, LiDAR mapping and processing, hardware troubleshooting, networking, and controller-related development.

Collision-avoidance leadership

Coordinated integration work where sensor behavior, localization, and vehicle response had to be evaluated together.

Project management + research

Translate faculty objectives into technical milestones while remaining hands-on with anomaly detection, localization health, and system testing.

Event-focused multimodal analysis

Instead of sending every camera frame and telemetry sample to a model, the workflow is organized around operational events. This reduces redundant inputs and preserves the context needed to investigate what changed around an abnormal condition.

Technical challenges

Most failures cross component boundaries.

  • Timing and rate: sensor messages arrive at different frequencies, and a slow consumer can make healthy data appear stale.
  • Localization health: a position estimate is not automatically trustworthy; its status and continuity matter to downstream analysis.
  • Distributed debugging: ROS 2 discovery, Docker networking, hardware interfaces, and host configuration can each interrupt the data path.
  • Multimodal context: camera information needs to correspond to the same operating window as the telemetry event.
  • Local inference constraints: on-system model serving trades cloud dependency for finite compute, memory, and latency budgets.
  • Team integration: parallel research threads need shared interfaces, test plans, and ownership boundaries before they can converge on the vehicle.

Decisions & tradeoffs

Prefer observable, bounded workflows over opaque automation.

  • Local model serving: keeps analysis close to the platform and reduces external dependency, but requires tighter resource management.
  • Event-based capture: cuts redundant multimodal inputs, but the trigger logic must preserve enough pre- and post-event context.
  • MOLA ICP migration: improves alignment with the platform's localization direction, while requiring integration, parameter evaluation, and new health signals.
  • Separate health checks from model interpretation: deterministic telemetry remains visible even when multimodal analysis is unavailable.
  • Incremental vehicle testing: slower than integrating everything at once, but safer and much easier to diagnose on a physical research platform.

Results / metrics

A stable sensor rate and an integrated analysis path.

  • Velodyne point-cloud processing was stabilized at approximately 10 Hz.
  • The anomaly-detection workflow combines vehicle telemetry, localization context, and camera information for abnormal-condition analysis.
  • Locally served LLM/VLM analysis is integrated through Ollama without claiming foundation-model training.
  • Project planning now connects research objectives to milestones, testing priorities, documentation, and integration ownership.

What I learned

Autonomy work rewards engineers who can move between layers.

JACart reinforced that the most valuable debugging question is often not “which node is broken?” but “where does the system's understanding stop matching reality?” Answering it can require reading ROS 2 topics, checking timestamps and transforms, inspecting localization health, validating container networking, and observing physical hardware.

Leading the project also changed how I plan. A technically interesting task is not automatically the next useful integration step. Clear interfaces, testable milestones, documentation, and a shared view of risk help a research team turn parallel experiments into one working platform.
